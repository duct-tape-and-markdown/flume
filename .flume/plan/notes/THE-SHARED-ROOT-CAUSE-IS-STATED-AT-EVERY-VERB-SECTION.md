# The clause's scope is now every verb — and the page holds ten hand copies of it

Shipped: the per-verb `docs/CLI.md` read is scoped by which `74` rows render
the shared state-root clause. That is all ten verbs (bay discovery runs ahead
of every one), so `status`, `tick`, `loop`, `log`, `check` and `friction` gained
the arms they were short of.

Two things for the next derive:

1. `SHARED_ROOT_ONLY_LEAD` is no longer a scope, only the non-vacuity witness
   that the scope is the clause's and not the lead's — asserted as
   `rendered.length > sharedOnly.length` (`tests/cliHelp.test.ts`). A verb that
   read no state root, or one that gained refusals of its own so that the lead
   set emptied, would red that line rather than the claim it guards. It is the
   honest reading today; a third marker would be the fix if the sets ever
   converge.

2. The page now spells one clause ten times in its own register — *Derived
   state is computed, never restated beside its source* over a doc surface.
   The help blocks render it from `SHARED_ROOT_CAUSES`; the page cannot, so the
   pin is what keeps the ten copies honest rather than removing them. Each copy
   differs only in its verb-specific tail ("ahead of the parse and the chain
   load", "rather than the queue path under it"), which is the part that is
   really the verb's. If that register ever becomes generable — a rendered
   include, or the page stating the clause once under
   *State-root and config-dir resolution* and each verb pointing at it — the
   per-verb read collapses to a pointer check and nine copies leave. Worth a
   question rather than an entry: it is a page-structure call, not a mechanical
   one, and the pin currently costs nothing.
