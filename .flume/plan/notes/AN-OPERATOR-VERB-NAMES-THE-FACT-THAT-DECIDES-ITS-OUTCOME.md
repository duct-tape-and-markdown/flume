# An operator verb names the fact that decides its outcome

Both halves shipped at the mechanism rather than at the two sites the entry
predicted.

**The stop flag's read has one home.** `loop`, `status` and now `wake` asked
the same question in three spellings; the probe and the sentence a failed one
names moved to `readStopFlag` (`src/cliStopFlag.ts`), and the long
proven-absence narration at `loop` and `status` shrank to a pointer. The
mid-run read in `src/waveTick.ts` stayed a bare `existsLoudUnder`: it wants
the throw, since a root it cannot stat mid-run must end the run. Declared at
the new module's header, not residue.

**`wake`'s unreadable-stop arm keeps exit 0.** The marker write already
landed, so an unstattable flag goes to stderr naming the path, the error and
that whether a stop is pending went unread — a declared degradation bounded by
`loop`'s own refusal on the same read. Deliberate, per the entry's "neither
verb's exit code moves". If the spec would rather `wake` take `EX_IOERR`
there, that is a human call, not a build one.

**The usage refusal is one rendering, adopted at nine sites.** `usageRefusal`
(`src/cliArgs.ts`) puts `refused: 'word'` above the usage line; `tick`, `stop`,
`wake`/`sleep`/`hold`, `check`, `log`, `loop`, `friction`, `render` and
`exclusive` all pass the words their own reading left standing. Two cases
needed a judgment call: a flag whose value was refused leaves the pair
standing, so the flag is named among the words (`--max`, `--phase`), and
`render`'s flag arm names `--entry` alone rather than the positional it would
have honored. Both are at their sites.

docs/CLI.md states the rendering once in the surface intro, with a
parenthetical in each verb's exit-2 sentence; the `wake` section gained the
printed line it never had.
